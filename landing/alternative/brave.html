Best Bookmark Alternative for Brave Browser (2026)

JR
Jeff R. · August 29, 2026 · 8 min read
Back to Alternatives

You switched to Brave for a reason: less tracking, fewer ads, more control over your data. But then you opened the bookmark manager — brave://bookmarks — and realized it's the same stripped-down Chromium bookmark tool you left behind in Chrome. If you've been searching for a bookmark alternative for Brave, this guide breaks down exactly where Brave's bookmarks fall short, what the real alternatives are in 2026, and which one fits best if privacy is why you're here in the first place.

Where Brave's Bookmark Manager Falls Short

Brave inherits Chromium's bookmark system almost untouched. It works, but "works" is a low bar for a browser whose entire pitch is doing better than the incumbents:

  • It's still the Chromium bookmark UI. Flat folders, one Favorites bar, and a search box. No tags, no saved filters, no saved views, no way to visualize a large library.
  • Brave Sync is device sync, not sharing. You can sync your own devices with a sync chain, but there's no way to share a curated set of links with a colleague or team. Exporting HTML files and emailing them is still the workaround.
  • Web URLs only. Brave bookmarks can't open a local project folder, a NAS share, or a file on your desktop. If half your daily resources live in Finder or Explorer, bookmarks can't help.
  • No usage insight. Brave blocks trackers on the web, but its bookmark manager can't tell you which of your 400 bookmarks you actually open — or how much time a better launcher would save you.
  • Extension dependence. The usual fix — install a cloud bookmarking extension — runs counter to why most people chose Brave. You're piping your entire browsing library through a third-party cloud service in the privacy browser.

The irony: Brave users are the most likely to care that a cloud bookmark manager uploads every link they save. That's exactly why local-first tools fit the Brave crowd best.

What to Look For in a Brave Bookmark Alternative

Before comparing tools, know the criteria that matter specifically for a Brave setup:

  1. Browser independence. The tool should live outside Brave so your library survives if you switch to Firefox, Safari, or anything else next year.
  2. Data control. Local-first storage, or at minimum a clear, honest data policy. No silent analytics on your link library.
  3. Beyond URLs. Support for local folders, network shares, and files — the things bookmarks simply cannot hold.
  4. Fast launching. Search and hotkeys that get you to a resource in under two seconds, not four clicks deep in a folder tree.
  5. Sharing that respects privacy. Team or group sharing without forcing your whole library into a public cloud.

1. JumpKit — the local-first launcher

JumpKit is a desktop app for Mac and Windows that organizes everything you open daily — web links, local folders, network shares, and files — into searchable, category-based "jumps." It launches any URL in your default browser, Brave included, and stores everything locally on your machine by default. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is tracked, and it works fully offline. For Brave users, that's the same philosophy that brought you to the browser, applied to your link library.

2. Raindrop.io — the polished cloud option

Raindrop.io is the best-known cloud bookmark manager, with a clean UI, good search, and extensions for every Chromium browser. If your links are all public-ish web pages and you want phone-to-desktop sync, it's a solid choice. The trade-off: your entire library lives on Raindrop's servers, and it can't open a local folder or shared drive.

3. Pocket — read-later, not launch-often

Pocket (now under Mozilla) is designed for saving articles to read later. It's great at that one job, but it isn't a launcher — no hotkeys, no folders or files, no team workflows. Brave users also often skip it since the Pocket integration story is Firefox-centric.

4. Booky — minimal and browser-agnostic

Booky gives you browser-independent bookmark lists on the web — useful if you hop between machines and just need your links anywhere. It's lightweight, but it's cloud-only, web-only, and has no local resource support or sharing model for teams.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a better way to bookmark for Brave?

Yes. If you use Brave's built-in manager to bookmark for Brave, you're limited to web URLs locked inside one browser bar. A local-first launcher like JumpKit is a stronger way to bookmark for Brave — it handles the URLs you'd normally bookmark, but also opens local folders, files, and shared network paths, keeps everything private by default, and follows you to any browser via your default setting.

What is the best bookmark alternative for Brave?

For Brave users, the best bookmark alternative depends on your priorities. JumpKit is the strongest option if you chose Brave for privacy — it is local-first, keeps your links on your machine, and also launches local folders and files. Raindrop.io is a good pick if you prefer cloud sync and don't mind your links living on a server.

Does Brave have a bookmark manager?

Yes — Brave uses the Chromium bookmark manager (chrome://bookmarks). It supports folders, bookmark bars, and Brave Sync, but it only stores web URLs, only works inside Brave, and has no team sharing, file access, or usage tracking.

Can I use the same bookmark tool in Brave and another browser?

Yes. Browser-independent tools like JumpKit run as desktop apps and open links in whatever your default browser is — Brave included — so your library follows you across every browser you use.

How do I export bookmarks from Brave?

Open Brave, go to brave://bookmarks, click the three-dot menu, and choose Export bookmarks. Brave saves a standard HTML bookmark file that most tools — including JumpKit — can read from as you rebuild your categories.

Is JumpKit privacy-friendly for Brave users?

Yes. JumpKit is local-first by default: nothing is uploaded, no analytics run on your links, and it works fully offline. That is the same privacy philosophy that attracts people to Brave in the first place.
